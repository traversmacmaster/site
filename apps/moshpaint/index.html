A photograph is pigment.

  1. Drop a photograph or video into the source wells.
  2. Choose Image brush or Mosh brush. Paint. Or use Place on Canvas to lay in the full image first.
  3. Pick RGB, Sort, or Melt to change your pigment.
  4. Use a damage brush to break marks already on the canvas.
  5. Try Wreck it. Undo anything. Replay everything.

Extended brushes: Tape pulls a narrow band; Shredder cuts seven strips; Chromatic Drag separates moving color; Edge Pull paints source structure; Time Smear blends captured moments.

Brush shapes: Round is familiar; Block makes hard tiles; Strip follows direction; Comb reveals parallel source teeth; Shard uses a repeatable seeded silhouette. Melt All affects the selection or every visible pixel, including shapes, text, placed images, and earlier effects.

Recorded gestures: Each finished stroke or mutation becomes a tile in the Performance strip. Click tiles to choose a subset; playback keeps their original order. All gestures clears that selection. Space previews from blank; Esc restores your finished artwork. Damage-only selections may have little effect without their earlier paint strokes. Undone tiles must be redone before replay. Arrow buttons reveal older tiles in long histories.

Video brushes capture source frames into their gestures for reliable replay. Mosh is a motion-driven pixel effect, not codec-level datamoshing. JPEG is a compression-inspired treatment. Wet Canvas keeps fresh marks reactive for a few seconds. Dry now stops it. Drying is recorded with the stroke; saving, replaying, and transforming freeze remaining wetness.

Living pigment ideas: Choose a video source and use Living field for a moving background or full-canvas texture. Use Video Select + Animate With for animated lettering, silhouettes, or matching color areas. Living brush paints moving ribbons; video shapes make collage windows. Freeze holds a moment, Range swaps the footage, and Bake turns it into pixels for damage brushes. Replay reveals living gestures in order.
